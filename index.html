Successful Salvage of Infected Tibial Shaft Non-union After Implant Removal with Ilizarov External Fixation: A Case Report

Dr Md SulaimanOrthopedic & Trauma Surgeon
05 Aug 20265 min read
On this page
Share WhatsApp Facebook

Infected tibial shaft non-union after internal fixation is one of the hardest problems in orthopedic trauma. This case report describes how a staged Ilizarov-based approach achieved union despite persistent infection, skin necrosis and a prolonged recovery.

The problem

When a fracture fails to heal after plate fixation and the site becomes infected, three things must be solved at once: the infection, the instability of the bone, and the failure of healing. Soft-tissue damage often makes each of them harder.

The Ilizarov circular external fixator is a well-established limb-salvage option here, because it holds the bone stable while allowing the infection and wound to be managed.

The case

A 26-year-old man presented with an infected non-union of the left tibial shaft after plate fixation. The operative wound had gradually broken down and the implant had become exposed.

Treatment approach

Treatment was staged. The implant was removed, the infected area was debrided, and the tibia was stabilized in an Ilizarov external fixator. Soft-tissue care continued through a complicated course that included skin necrosis and later skin grafting.

Union is achievable even when infection and soft-tissue problems persist, if treatment is staged and followed through.

Outcome

Follow-up radiographs showed maintained alignment with the frame in place, then progressing union. After the frame was removed, the final radiographs showed satisfactory union of the tibial shaft.

Clinical message

Ilizarov external fixation remains an effective limb-salvage option for infected tibial shaft non-union after implant removal, even when severe infection, skin necrosis and prolonged wound problems occur.
